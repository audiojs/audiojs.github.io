A tone generator for tuning, testing and listening

Play a sine to tune by ear or check your hearing, a square or sawtooth for a buzzier test signal, a sweep to find what rattles in a speaker, pink noise to level a room. The sound is made live by the browser's Web Audio API5. The saved file is the same sound rendered offline and written by @audio/encode. Nothing is uploaded.

Notes and hertz

Notes follow twelve-tone equal temperament: a semitone is a frequency ratio of 21/12, a cent 21/1200. A4 is 440 Hz by ISO 166 and MIDI note 69, middle C is note 607, so C4 is 440 × 2−9/12 = 261.63 Hz. Set A4 to 415, 432 or 442 Hz and the names follow; the frequency stays as typed. The field shows hundredths of a hertz, and that is exactly what plays.

Additive synthesis

A periodic sound is a sum of sine waves at whole multiples of its frequency. The Additive tab sets the first sixteen. Odd harmonics at 1/n make a square wave, every harmonic at 1/n a sawtooth, odd harmonics at 1/n² with alternating sign a triangle5. The organ preset pulls out all nine drawbars of a Hammond organ. They are named in feet after organ pipes, and a drawbar of f feet sounds 16/f times the 16′ pitch: 16′, 5⅓′, 8′, 4′, 2⅔′, 2′, 1⅗′, 1⅓′ and 1′ are harmonics 1, 3, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 168. The browser plays the sum as one oscillator from a PeriodicWave, scaled to a peak of 1 before the level5.

Binaural beats

Two tones a few hertz apart, one in each ear, are heard as one tone pulsing at the difference: 245 Hz on the left and 255 Hz on the right beat at 10 Hz. The pulse is not in the air. It comes from the hearing system combining the two ears' signals, first in the superior olivary complex, and it is heard with carriers below about 1000 Hz, best around a few hundred, for differences up to about 30 Hz1. Every current browser can play it: separate channels through the Web Audio API work in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari15. The output decides. A mono setting, one earbud or a speaker mixes both tones into both ears, and the binaural beat becomes an ordinary one2.

What binaural beats do beyond being heard is not settled. A meta-analysis of 22 studies found a medium overall effect (g = 0.45) on memory, attention, anxiety and pain, depending on the beat frequency, the exposure time and whether it came before or during the task9. A systematic review of 14 studies that recorded EEG asked whether brain rhythms follow the beat: five found they did, eight found they did not, one was mixed1. This page makes no health claim.

Noise and sweeps

White noise has equal power at every frequency. Pink noise falls 3 dB per octave, so every octave carries the same power; here it is white noise through Paul Kellet's filter, accurate to ±0.05 dB above 9.2 Hz at 44.1 kHz11. Brown noise falls 6 dB per octave; here a leaky integrator, flat below 20 Hz instead of rising further, so less of the speaker's travel goes to rumble you cannot hear. Measured on 22 seconds of each from 125 Hz to 8 kHz: 0.0, −3.0 and −6.0 dB per octave.

A logarithmic sweep gives every octave the same time: f(t) = f0 (f1/f0)t/T, a curve the browser follows sample by sample5. It is the swept sine used to measure the impulse response and distortion of speakers and rooms14. A linear sweep gives every hertz the same time.

Levels and safe listening

The level is the peak in dBFS, decibels below digital full scale. The default, −18 dBFS, is the EBU alignment level, 18 dB below the maximum coding level13. A sine's RMS sits 3 dB below its peak. Square, sawtooth and triangle come from the browser's own oscillator, which may drop harmonics to prevent aliasing5; measured in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, their peak then sits up to 2 dB off the level at high frequencies.

dBFS is not loudness: that depends on your volume and your speakers or headphones. The WHO's safe-listening standard allows adults 80 dB for 40 hours a week10, and a test tone at full volume in headphones can be far above it. The ear is most sensitive between about 2 and 5 kHz4, so a sweep at a steady level swells there. The upper limit of hearing falls with age, from the highest frequencies down12; a tone you no longer hear still reaches your ears and your speakers.

The same sounds in code

The audio library makes these in a line. A tone is a function of time, audio.from(t => 0.25 * Math.sin(2 * Math.PI * 440 * t), { duration: 2 }); a sweep is a generator. Edit the numbers below and play.

Questions

Which browser supports binaural beats?

Every current browser: Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on computers and phones, can send a separate tone to each ear through the Web Audio API this page uses. What stops binaural beats is the output: a mono audio setting, one earbud, a laptop or phone speaker, or a Bluetooth speaker. Press Check stereo in the Binaural tab; you should hear the left ear, then the right.

Do binaural beats work?

You do hear the beat. Whether it does more is not settled. A 2019 meta-analysis of 22 studies found a medium overall effect on memory, attention, anxiety and pain, and a 2023 systematic review of 14 EEG studies found the evidence that brain waves follow the beat inconsistent. This page makes no health claim.

How do I play an exact frequency?

Type it into the big number: decimals are fine, from 1 Hz to 20 kHz. Or type a note name such as A4 or C♯3 and press Enter. Up and down arrows in the frequency field move 1 Hz, left and right on the slider move 1 cent, and ÷2 and ×2 jump an octave.

What is the difference between white, pink and brown noise?

White noise has equal power at every frequency, so it sounds bright. Pink noise falls 3 dB per octave, giving every octave equal power; it is the usual signal for speaker and room tuning. Brown noise falls 6 dB per octave and sounds like a low rumble.

Log or linear sweep?

A logarithmic sweep spends the same time on every octave, so 20 to 40 Hz takes as long as 10 to 20 kHz; it is the sweep used to measure speakers and rooms. A linear sweep spends the same time on every hertz and passes the bass in a blink.

Can I download the tone?

Yes. Save file renders the current sound to WAV (16 or 24-bit), FLAC or MP3 at 44.1 or 48 kHz, up to 10 minutes, with a 10 ms fade at each end so it starts and stops without a click. A sweep is saved at its own length. Nothing is uploaded.

What does the level in dBFS mean?

The peak of the waveform in decibels below digital full scale: −6 dBFS is half of full scale. The default, −18 dBFS, is the EBU alignment level. It sets the signal, not how loud it is in the room. A sine wave's RMS level is 3 dB below its peak; square, sawtooth and triangle can peak up to 2 dB off the level at high frequencies, where the browser drops harmonics to prevent aliasing.

Why can I not hear a high tone?

The highest frequency you can hear falls with age, from the top down, and small speakers may not play it at all. Do not raise the level to find a tone you cannot hear: your ears and speakers still get all of it.
